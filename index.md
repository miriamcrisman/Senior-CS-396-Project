## About me: 
Hello! I'm Miriam Crisman, a senior majoring in Data Science and Political Science with minors in German and Statistics. My current fascination is with all types of political polarization and how they relate to public opinion, elections, and the news media. 

## Vision Statement: 
My goal for this project is to implement the theoretical trifold model that Professor Araujo and I developed this summer into an agent-based model in AnyLogic. I hope to look at and understand the different types of polarization that emerge, how behaviors, opinions, and sentiments spread in relationships and broader networks, how different traits of individuals affect interactions between agents, and what kind of ways there might be to prevent or slow the spread of polarization. Looking at church communities in the Global South is of particular importance because so much of the current literature focuses very heavily on the United States. This project will expand our knowledge of polarization into new communities, and give a more quantitative perspective on an often very qualitative field. 

## Summary: 
I am using agent-based modeling to take a look at how polarization spreads in communities that meet on a regular schedule (like a church community). The goal is to figure out what things can lead to an increase in polarization. I will also be writing a short literature review of what work has been done on polarization in the Global South up to now. I hope by the end of the year to have a fully functioning model that can be used by people without too much experience in coding to see what changes in different attributes and characteristics can do to a community.
